⚠ LEGAL, LICENSING AND INTERFERENCE — read before you transmit

SignalMatrix can transmit: on amateur-radio frequencies (APRS, packet, Winlink, DMR, M17, JS8Call, CW/digital modes via CAT control), on licence-free ISM bands (MeshCore, Meshtastic, LoRa), and across a telephone/VoIP gateway — and it can do so automatically and unattended (bots, beacons, APRS objects, bridge rules, auto-acks, scheduled bulletins).

  • You, the operator and station licensee, are responsible for everything your station sends, including anything this software sends on your behalf. Nothing here is legal advice.
  • Interference. A misconfigured or runaway station can block other users, including emergency traffic. Use simulation mode or a dummy load first, start at low power, listen before transmitting, and never transmit where you are not authorised.
  • Penalties. In the US, violating FCC rules can bring fines (often thousands of dollars per violation) and licence revocation. “The software did it” is not a defence.
  • Amateur transmissions need a valid licence and your own call sign (47 CFR 97.119); automatic control, bridging and third-party traffic have their own rules (97.109, 97.113, 97.115, 97.221) — no business traffic and no encrypted content on amateur bands.
  • Licence-free LoRa radios must use the correct regional settings and accept interference. Phone gateways, Winlink, APRS-IS, DAPNET, DMR/M17 networks and data providers have their own terms — get your own accounts and keys and honour rate limits.
  • Receive-only data (AIS, ADS-B, GOES, satellites): never transmit on those services; do not decode, store or publish private traffic.
  • Messages, positions, logs and the config file are personal and secret data — protect them.
  • SignalMatrix is a coordination aid. It is not certified, is not a substitute for 911/EAS or official channels, and may fail, lose or delay messages. It is provided as is, without warranty of any kind.
